# Zorro II termination coupon measurement and decision plan

**Status: designs checked in CAD. No boards, measured waveforms, receiver timing margins or hardware acceptance results exist for these coupons.** A passing coupon would qualify a candidate for a loaded motherboard prototype. Absolute device limits and system timing still govern the production decision.

## Fixture and reference

Build separate A, B and C assemblies with the same board lot, socket, load configuration, supply, source board, cable, ground attachment, instrument settings and ambient temperature. A retains R3.1 private traces on all nine lines with short socket escape adjustments. B places one fully loaded nine-way 1 kΩ SIP at the end of nine radial capacitors. C has nine separate 1 kΩ DIN0204 axials after identical radial capacitors. Each private RC net has precisely two component endpoints. All fixture sockets use 1.02 mm finished holes on 4.85 mm rows, 1.53 mm external lands and 1.80 mm internal lands. The larger hole differs from the production socket drill and changes local parasitics in A as well. The resistor ground connection, package parasitics and branch geometry vary deliberately. B puts all nine lines on one common lead for simultaneous-switching stress. The motherboard's six-pack map distributes these lines differently. The coupon's BD1 resistor is accordingly part of the SIP stress case even though motherboard B+ proposes a discrete R605.

The 0.25 mm tracks run side by side on F.Cu over In1 ground. The common parallel section is 88 mm long on 0.65 mm centres. The signal returns also see ground pours on the outside. In2 is the external +5 V plane. The KiCad project uses R3.1's nominal dielectric layers of 0.48 mm each, 35 µm copper and FR4 εr 4.5. Ask the fabricator for its **actual pressed stackup** and measure delay and impedance on the lot. A single uninterrupted test plane omits H7, motherboard power splits, other slot stubs, card drivers and the EATX card cage. Production baseline measurements on a working machine remain necessary. ## Characterise the source before comparing packages

1. On a working R3.1 system capture rising and falling edges at the driving package and far slot, noting actual device suffix, bus ownership and OE/direction. Include CPU accesses and a bus-mastering card. For ISA use a bridgeboard and test separately. Freeze the motherboard revision, fitted cards, Agnus, oscillator, supply voltage, ambient and probe capacitance.
2. Start with the R3.1 HCT245 5 V transceiver family on a short external driver board or an equivalent calibrated pattern source. Measure driven high/low voltage, loaded 10–90% edge, skew and source impedance from two known loads. Reproduce the observed edges at J1. For sensitivity only, sweep 2–8 ns or the wider measured range. A nominal 50 Ω generator that produces 5 V into open circuit behaves differently when terminated. Verify at J1 before each series. [TI HCT245 limits](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/sn74hct245.pdf)
3. Use the same active probes (target ≤1 pF), at least 1 GHz bandwidth and 5 GS/s for the 2 ns trial. Calibrate, deskew near/far channels from one edge, and record residual skew, vertical uncertainty, timebase and probe C. Measure quiet-ground noise and repeat A before and after B/C to bound drift. Cross-check any spike with a shorter return or differential probe. Save raw waveforms and fixture photographs.
4. With power off inspect socket insertion, confirm isolation of each RC junction and check for supply/ground shorts. On B verify pin 1 to ground, approximately 1 kΩ from each private lead to common and approximately 2 kΩ between any two private leads with DC caps open. On A/C verify each junction through its own resistor. Record R/C values as fitted and component height/lead forming. On power-up observe at J1.20 and C10.
5. On each load and assembly drive each line alone. Hold the victim first low, then high while immediate neighbours toggle together and in opposite directions. Drive all nine together and alternating, on both polarities. Include repeated bursts and low repetition steps with at least 5 µs recovery for the nominal 1 µs RC time constant. Repeat with minimum and maximum measured slew. Sweep the **common allowed operating supply/temperature range** of the actual driver and receivers. Record failing corners individually.
6. On a separately approved motherboard prototype, repeat at near and far **receiver pins** under empty, representative and full slot occupancy, long cards, ownership handover, interrupts and DMA. Measure actual read/write strobes, data, arbitration and configuration paths including CN601.95. Verify timing at the real latch edge. A passive coupon has no bus transaction controller. [Commodore expansion bus electrical and timing description](https://www.theflatnet.de/pub/cbm/amiga/AmigaDevDocs/hard_k.html)

## Acceptance worksheet

Before testing, enter the fitted driver and each receiver's guaranteed VIH(min), VIL(max), absolute input limits, allowed clamp current, setup, hold and required pulse widths at the test corner. Use the more restrictive connected-device limit. HCT thresholds of 2.0 V and 0.8 V are applicable examples for the stated HCT supply range. A 50% crossing alone is insufficient. If a legacy part lacks a documented value, record the unknown and withhold system sign-off for that path.

| Quantity | Record | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| Overshoot and undershoot | Vmax, Vmin relative to local receiver GND and supply, duration outside each rail and repeatability. | Remain within actual device limits including measurement uncertainty. If excursions rely on clamp conduction, establish current from a validated model or instrumented fixture and meet per-pin and aggregate limits. |
| Ringing and crosstalk | First and subsequent peaks, peak-to-peak, decay and threshold crossings on active and quiet victim. | One valid transition for each commanded edge. No return across a valid threshold during the sample window. A quiet high stays above VIH and a quiet low below VIL including uncertainty. |
| Settling | Last crossing into the valid logic band and time until the waveform stays within ±5% of its measured final step amplitude. Capture a long enough window to include RC recovery. | Valid before the earliest receiver setup deadline and through hold. Record the ±5% metric for comparison. A later tail outside ±5% can coexist with a valid logic level and must be assessed against the actual application. |
| Timing margin | For each actual capture edge compute setup margin = capture time − last valid data time − required setup. Hold margin = first subsequent invalid data time − capture time − required hold. Include reference skew and repeatability. | Lower bounds on both margins positive after timing uncertainty and approved system guardband. Verify pulse widths and propagation in both directions. Without a named receiver, strobe, budget and guardband, record comparative results only. |
| Common ground and supply | RN1.1 or discrete return versus local plane, supply droop, simultaneous-edge displacement and quiet-line level. | Worst measured displacement leaves positive VIH/VIL margin and meets supply limits at each load. Reject a common return that creates false crossings or exhausts measured timing margin. |
| A/B/C decision | Tabulate per-lane worst case and uncertainty, with before/after A measurements. | Accept a package only where absolute and timing criteria pass. Any repeatable degradation beyond measurement uncertainty needs explicit review against measured system headroom. Preserve A on an unresolved branch. |

Record assembly/lot, lane, configuration, load, driver/OE direction, pattern, voltage, temperature, probes, threshold source, edge polarity, peak/trough, ringing crossings, settling, setup/hold, uncertainty, raw capture path and disposition for **each** run. Prior idealised settling traces are sensitivity studies. They supply no measured acceptance margin.
