# Original A2000 Rev 6.2 engineering comparison

The expansion banks on Commodore's design use **individual 1 nF series capacitors, each feeding one resistor to ground**. Many resistors share the ground lead of a SIP. The EATX Zorro bank keeps the individual capacitor junctions and implements the resistors separately. The most consequential differences for B+ are RP604's disputed value, the two original discrete returns and the physically separate EATX grant termination. A single donor's stuffing cannot establish the specification for every 6.x machine.

## What the original placement supports

On 312723, five Zorro connectors run parallel to one another. The two-row strip of discrete capacitor sites and the long SIP outlines lie outboard of the expansion bank. ISA has its own capacitor strip and SIP groups beside the ISA connectors. The CPU and Kickstart sit across from expansion logic, with DRAM in repeated banks, bypasses beside DIP loads, port filters along the I/O edge and rail reservoirs near power distribution. The bare copper images show rows of plated sites, lateral signal bundles on the component side, long orthogonal runs on the solder side and wide copper areas interrupted by those routes. Their registration and board edge differ slightly between scans. Use the factory drawing to locate references and the scans to inspect local geometry. Do not dimension an EATX connector from a JPEG. [Factory placement, printed p. 3](https://amiga.net.au/files/Tech_Amiga/Commodore_System_Schematics_A2000_Revision_6_ocr.pdf)

A direct check on the component-side donor scan found ten hole centres in the outboard Zorro strip near image X = 278 pixels, from Y = 4,772.6 to 5,312.3 pixels (origin at upper left). Nine intervals span 539.7 pixels. At the stated 600 dpi that gives **2.54 mm mean pitch**. This supports the 0.100-inch SIP lead pattern independently of the CAD reconstruction. It gives no precise finished-hole size. The replica file headers also identify the extensionless file in the 1.8.5 ZIP as inch Excellon (M48) and the top and bottom copper as metric Gerber artwork. [Donor scan and archive](https://github.com/Floppie209/Amiga2000-remake/tree/25b3270bd8dbb06ab34206df5b2986971b86b263)

The extracted footprint register gives each indexed historical part, body library, position, angle, pads 1–2 separation where available, and same-label EATX counterpart. A same-label comparison is a lookup aid. For example, U503 belongs to an eight-DRAM bank in the original, while Jason uses the number for a wide SRAM. In the replica, many 1 nF capacitors have 10 mm holes and a rectangular library body. The factory parts list identifies these parts as **axial multilayer ceramic**. A B+ 2.50 mm radial ceramic is an electrically qualified space-saving substitution. Axial MLCs, where sourced and accommodated, have stronger historical evidence. The crosswalk follows all **91** fitted EATX expansion RC paths end to end. For each, the capacitor signal end was matched to an actual connector pad in the reconstruction and on the EATX, and the private end to exactly one resistor element. Historical ISA numeric pad numbering was translated to lettered contacts using the two connector sections. All 91 contacts matched. This verifies the logical comparison across these files. It does not prove that the replicas reproduce every donor trace. In particular, the two Zorro resistor values above must be read against the factory documents. The reconstructed schematic identifies 39 fitted or optional network symbols. Its bussed-network and isolated-pack symbol types agree with the recorded element count and topology on all 39. RPF603/RPF604 are later rework, and RP900/RP901 are omitted from the KiCad reconstruction. Agreement within a reconstruction cannot settle a donor stuffing value. ## Power, ground and footprint transfer

The donor routes supplies across its two visible copper faces and uses broad copper interspersed with signal channels. Slot distribution and plated links share that space. R3.1 has a 304.800 × 330.200 mm outline, outer signal/ground pours, In1 ground with 58 tracks and In2 split power with 135 tracks. ATX standby, generated −5 V, memory supply and analogue filtering introduce distinct current paths. Copying the replica's rail widths, antipad clearances, bus lengths or ground layout would provide no assurance of equal impedance, rail drop or return inductance. Keep the R3.1 stack and connector lands. Check return continuity beneath each revised bank, thermal spokes, rail necks, card load and bypass loop after routing. [R3.1 stack](PCB.md) · [Baseline routing and drill screen](THT_LAYOUT_FEASIBILITY.md)

| Transfer class | Suitable uses and conditions |
|---|---|
| Reuse existing EATX footprints | Production CPU/chipset sockets, ROM, keyed connectors and retained SIPs where pin count, pair/common map, pitch and body are already right. Compare SIP end mark and rail explicitly. Keep all 11 mounting centres and all EATX connector datums. |
| Adapt the historical package | 2.54 mm SIP rows, conventional axial resistors, radial electrolytics and selected DIPs. Confirm lead form, finished hole and pad annulus, polarity/pin numbering, body clearance and electrical performance against the proposed modern part. Use the R3.1 −12 V reservoir polarity. |
| Require a fresh electrical or mechanical design | SRAM and its bus interfaces, selectable Agnus, clock damping, analogue filters, ATX standby and −5 V, rear I/O, ISA row geometry, port feedthroughs and under-card sites. The full comparison and approval items are linked below. |

The historical comparison supports the B+ proposal and coupon evaluation. Production implementation awaits review of this comparison, the design and the measured results.
