# ATX standby timing trial

R9101 is already a verified 6.2 kΩ axial resistor. D9100 is a 1N4148 with cathode on C9104 pin 1 and U9101 pin 1. Its anode joins the switch J9100, R9100 and R9101 pin 2. C9104 is 10 µF from the inverter input to ground. R9100 supplies the switch node from +5VSB through 30 kΩ. The separate low-side Q9100 drives PS-ON from the latched output. [Accepted Q9100 layout](../resumed-atx-driver/README.md)

Closing the switch grounds the diode anode. C9104 then discharges through 6.2 kΩ with the diode reverse-biased. The ideal time constant is 62 ms. At 4.5 V initial charge and 0.5–1.2 V falling thresholds, crossing takes approximately 82–136 ms at nominal R and C. Opening the switch forward-biases the diode while there is sufficient voltage across R9101. The charging resistance is dominated by the 30 kΩ pull-up, giving a 300 ms RC scale initially and 362 ms with the diode off. The exact interval depends on forward drop, leakage, capacitance under bias, rail ramp and the Schmitt thresholds. A 1 µA change in leakage can shift the final node voltage by 36.2 mV. The SMD timing capacitor is specified by value and package. Its original MPN and biased capacitance are unknown. A polar replacement requires checking reversal during standby collapse. [HCT14 thresholds](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/sn74hct14.pdf)

The isolated placement and route search tested a 7.62 mm horizontal diode and a 5 mm radial capacitor with 2 mm lead pitch using the installed resistor as the common-node anchor. Neither front nor back escapes produced a solution with the existing surrounding copper and front component envelopes. A compact upright diode trial at the existing switch-node copper also failed the placement screen. D9100 and C9104 stay surface-mount. The existing switch and resistor traces and verified Q9100 remain intact.

Reopen the group with a measured press/release and standby-collapse envelope at U9101 pin 1. Record +5VSB, the switch, the Schmitt output and PS-ON during cold start, contact bounce, power loss and repeated cycling. A group relocation can then be tested against those limits.
